## What was built

The owner can now use Executor's **"Through your agent"** (`model`) and **"In your client"** (`native`) modes. Any MCP
server's question reaches the owner as a t3 card, and no agent can answer it.

- **One path, in the MCP host** (`apps/environment/src/mcp/host.ts`, `mcp/elicitation.ts`). Every agent call declares
  elicitation, with no per-server switch. The engine's `onInput` turns each question into a card spec. The caller shows
  the card and returns the owner's answer, and the host turns the answer back into the server's `ElicitResult`.
- **Cards are t3's own:**

  | Server's question | t3 card |
  | --- | --- |
  | a confirmation (a form with no fields) | `mcp-elicitation` approval |
  | a page (URL mode) | `mcp-elicitation` approval, showing the link and its site; "Opened it" accepts |
  | an Executor `approval-required` | `mcp-elicitation` approval |
  | a form with fields, or an Executor `input-required` | `user_input`, one question per field plus a Send / Decline question |

  Every card names the server and shows the agent's call.
- **Durable agent:** the `mcp` extension passes an `asker` with `mcpCall` over Workers RPC. The Session holds the card
  in memory (`extensions/mcpQuestions.ts`) and pushes it as a pending approval or question. The differ renders it, and
  t3's answer resolves it. Nothing is written to the Session's storage.
- **CLI agents:** the relay passes the calling thread's asker (`cliQuestionAsker`). The CLI wrapper raises the card on
  the live turn, the way tool cards are raised, and now also raises `user_input` cards answered with t3's `answers`. The
  CLI's HTTP request is held meanwhile.
- **Owner only** (`mcp/ownerOnly.ts`): a form card's id carries `t3flare-elicit`. The Environment refuses
  `t3_pending_request` `read` and `respond` on such ids, on `/mcp` and on the Durable `mcp()` RPC, before t3 sees the
  request. Approval cards were already out of an agent's reach.
- **"Through your agent":** a server whose `resume` takes `requestId` and `response` is a **pausing server**.
  - Its `resume` is hidden from agents and refused if called.
  - A pause in a result becomes a card showing Executor's message, its `_meta` (app, tool, arguments) and the agent's
    call. t3flare then calls `resume` itself.
  - `accept` is sent only on the owner's approval. A decline is sent as `{ action: "decline" }` (Executor's own decline
    response), and an unanswered card as `{ action: "cancel" }`. Nothing is left to expire.
  - At most 16 rounds. The agent gets only the final result, and a `requestId` in an expired outcome is masked.
- **cloudpid ADR-0041's rules, as ported:**
  - Ported: owner only; declared on every agent call; a credential form or an unsafe page declined unseen; malformed
    and over-bound forms refused.
  - Ported in part: answers are kept nowhere in t3flare, but t3's event log keeps a `user_input` card's answers, as it
    does for every question (platform).
  - Not ported: the Secrets check (t3flare Sessions have none).
  - Prompt injection is not used as the reason for any rule.
- **Fixture** (`scripts/tools/fixtureMcp.ts`), with Legacy questions on the call's SSE stream:
  - `fixture_ask`, `fixture_confirm`, `fixture_page`, `fixture_password`;
  - `/executor-mcp`: `call`, `execute` and `resume`, Executor's model mode in miniature.

## Limits checked

- **Wait:** a question waits up to 10 minutes, cloudpid's Legacy wait and the CLI card's `CLI_ASK_MS`. The engine pauses
  the call's 270 s bound while a question is pending (`McpEngineHooks.onInput`).
- **Workers:** a Worker or Durable Object waiting on I/O has no wall-clock limit while its caller is connected. The CLI
  tool timeout is 900 s.
- **Commissary's 240 s cap (SPI-1083)** does not bind a question. Commissary's own approval questions are Modern rounds,
  each a new request, and Commissary forwards no upstream elicitation.
- **Restarts:** a restart ends a waiting call, and its card closes as cancelled. Questions that survive a restart, and
  `-32042` pages, are SPI-1093.

## Deviations from the plan

- **The form card's context leads its first question.** A `user_input` item has no prompt of its own, so the server's
  message and the agent's call lead the first question. The Send question names the server.
- **The fence also refuses `read`**, not only `respond`. `list` still lists the id; that is t3's answer.
- **The Environment Worker now imports `@cloudpid/mcp/elicitation-url`** for the URL checks. It is SDK-free, and
  `sdk-isolation.test.ts` now allows it and checks its graph.

## Code review (vs `origin/main`, two axes, by the implementer, one after the other)

**Standards** (AGENTS.md, ADR-0001, ADR-0030, the repo's comment and diagnostics conventions, the smell baseline):

- t3code untouched. No Layer, Shadow or Patch: the wrapper's cards and the `/mcp` fence are in our own code (U37, U51).
- The toolchain was run from the repo root only.
- **Fixed in review:** the host recorded a card's key for the server's own withdrawal (`onInputEnded`) only after the
  owner answered, so a withdrawal mid-wait left the card open. It is now kept before the wait, with a test.
- **Fixed in review:** two import statements from `@cloudpid/mcp/types` in `host.ts` merged into one.
- Judgement calls, kept:
  - *Duplicated Code*: the approval options and the answer typing are built in `elicitation.ts` only. The Session
    (`McpQuestions`) and the wrapper only render.
  - *Data Clumps*: `QuestionContext` (server, tool, arguments, own host) bundles what travels with a question.
  - *Middle Man*: `cliQuestionAsker` mostly delegates to the wrapper's asker. It holds the withdrawal and the timeout,
    as `approveCliTool` does.
  - `redactedResult` replaces a result whose `structuredContent` still holds a `requestId` with its message, as
    pi-mcp-adapter does. A completed Executor result that echoed its `requestId` would lose its body; U52 watches the
    contract.

**Spec** (SPI-1079 and its comments, the owner's brief, root's two additions):

- Covered: elicitation in form and URL mode on Durable and CLI agents, using t3's own cards.
- Covered: "Through your agent" approvals resume only after the owner's card. `resume` is t3flare's alone, and the card
  shows Executor's own text and the agent's call.
- Covered: a decline sends Executor's decline, and an approve is never sent without the owner.
- Covered: cloudpid's rules are ported, and the reasons for each are recorded.
- Covered: the 240 s cap and the Worker limits are checked; stage `spi1079` is in `STAGES`.
- "In your client" works through case 1. Its approvals for `call` are t3flare's existing write card, so on a
  full-access thread a native-mode `call` runs without one. That is stock t3's meaning of full access, recorded as a
  consequence in ADR-0028. Approvals inside `execute` still ask.
- Not proven live against Executor (owner away): SPI-1092.
- No scope creep found. The fence's `read` refusal goes slightly beyond "answer"; it follows cloudpid's "no tool to
  list or answer".

No blocking findings remain.
