## Q1. Do pi-ai's sign-in flows run in workerd?

**They load.** Evidence: [`q1-environment.json`](spi-1161/q1-environment.json).
- In the agents Worker (`nodejs_compat`), `process.versions.node` is `22.23.4`.
- `node:http`'s `createServer` exists, and `listen(1455, "127.0.0.1")` answers "listening" with an address, but nothing can reach that server.
- `node:crypto`'s `randomBytes` and `node:zlib`'s `zstdCompressSync` exist, and so does `WebSocket`.
- `openai-chatgpt.js` imports `node:http` and `node:crypto` at its top level. It loaded without error.

**Bundling needs one call.** pi-ai's `auth/oauth/load.js` imports each flow through a variable specifier, which a
bundler cannot follow, and the Rolldown build leaves those imports unresolved. Calling `registerBunOAuthFlows()` from
`@earendil-works/pi-ai/bun-oauth` once at module load registers all nine flows statically, and the bundle then holds
them. `registerBundledOAuthFlowLoaders` would allow registering fewer flows, but pi-ai does not export it (only
`./bun-oauth` is exported). The cost is that the providers we don't use are bundled too.

**Browser methods:** both start, and fall back to a paste prompt
([`q1-codex-browser-start.json`](spi-1161/q1-codex-browser-start.json),
[`q1-anthropic-browser-start.json`](spi-1161/q1-anthropic-browser-start.json)). Because `listen` seems to succeed,
pi-ai believes a callback server is running, and Anthropic's browser method sends `localhost:53692` as its redirect.
Only the device-code and copy-code methods should be offered.

**Device codes work as shipped.**
- ChatGPT: a code from `auth.openai.com/api/accounts/deviceauth/usercode`. The owner enters it at
  `https://auth.openai.com/codex/device`. It lasts 15 min and is polled every 5 s. The exchange at
  `auth.openai.com/oauth/token`, with redirect `https://auth.openai.com/deviceauth/callback`, succeeded from a
  Worker. Recorded on the custom-zone probe: polled from a Durable Object's alarm every 5 s, approved and exchanged
  30 s after the start, six polls ([`q3-codex-zone-probe.json`](spi-1161/q3-codex-zone-probe.json) `signIn`; the token
  response's fields were `access_token, earliest_refresh_at, expires_in, id_token, refresh_token, scope, token_type`).
  In the stage rounds pi-ai's own `login()` completed it too (observed, not recorded; the stored credential's shape is
  in [`q3-codex-plan.json`](spi-1161/q3-codex-plan.json) and the Q5 files).
- Copilot: `github.com/login/device`, polled from the Worker until authorized; the next step,
  `api.github.com/copilot_internal/v2/token`, answered `403 subscription_ended`. Observed, not recorded.
  [`finish-logout-github-copilot.json`](spi-1161/finish-logout-github-copilot.json) has empty `attempts` because no
  credential was ever stored; it is not evidence of this.

**pi-ai's `login()` cannot be relied on in a Durable Object.** It keeps the PKCE verifier, the state, and its wait for
the paste in memory.
- Recorded for round 3: the owner's pastes at 10:56:06Z were refused with `409 no flow is waiting for a paste`
  ([`q1-anthropic-paste.json`](spi-1161/q1-anthropic-paste.json), [`q2-openai-paste.json`](spi-1161/q2-openai-paste.json)).
- Observed, not recorded: those copy-code and paste-URL flows had started at about 10:47:47Z, so they were gone within
  about 8 minutes, though the probe kept an alarm firing every 15 s.
- Observed, not recorded: the same happened to the paste flows of rounds 1 and 2, which waited much longer; the alarm
  counter read 151 in round 3; round 3's device flow survived because it finished in about 6 minutes.
- A redeploy also drops every running login (by construction: the login is a promise in the isolate's memory).

**What the sign-in tickets must build** (the probe's `startDurable` and `pasteDurable` in `4cd011c` are a working
sketch):
- **Start.** Make the PKCE verifier (32 random bytes, base64url) and the challenge (S256). Keep `{verifier, state,
  startedAt}` in the Environment DO's storage under the flow's id, and return the authorize URL.
  - **Anthropic:** `https://claude.ai/oauth/authorize?code=true&client_id=<pi-ai's>&response_type=code&redirect_uri=https://platform.claude.com/oauth/code/callback&scope=org:create_api_key user:profile user:inference user:sessions:claude_code user:mcp_servers user:file_upload&code_challenge=…&code_challenge_method=S256&state=<verifier>`.
    pi-ai uses the verifier as the state; we kept that.
  - **ChatGPT device code:** keep `device_auth_id` and `user_code`, and poll
    `auth.openai.com/api/accounts/deviceauth/token` from the DO's alarm rather than in a loop. A 403 or 404 means
    pending. On success the poll returns `{authorization_code, code_verifier}`; exchange them at `/oauth/token` with
    pi-ai's client id and the device redirect. The endpoints are from pi-ai 1.5.0's source; the alarm poll itself was
    tested on the custom-zone probe (above).
  - **Copilot:** keep `device_code`, and poll `github.com/login/oauth/access_token` from the alarm. Derived from
    pi-ai 1.5.0's source; the alarm form is untested for Copilot.
- **Paste (Anthropic).** Split the pasted `code#state`, check the state, and POST JSON to
  `https://platform.claude.com/v1/oauth/token` with `{grant_type: "authorization_code", client_id, code, state,
  redirect_uri, code_verifier}`. It completed 182 s after the start
  ([`q1-anthropic-paste-durable.json`](spi-1161/q1-anthropic-paste-durable.json)).
  - The response has the fields `access_token`, `account`, `expires_in` (28800), `organization`, `refresh_token`,
    `refresh_token_expires_in`, `scope`, `token_type`, `token_uuid`.
  - The granted scope **lacks `org:create_api_key`**.
  - The access token starts `sk-ant-oat01-` and the refresh token `sk-ant-ort01-`.
- **Store.** Write the credential in pi-ai's `OAuthCredential` shape (`{type: "oauth", access, refresh, expires,
  …extras}`; ChatGPT adds `accountId`), so that pi-ai's `refresh` and `toAuth` (`provider.auth.oauth`) and
  `Models.getAuth` work on it unchanged. That part of pi-ai works in workerd and is worth keeping.
- **Accept the paste loosely.** Expect a code or a URL pasted from a phone, with or without its scheme.
  [`q2-openai-paste-durable.json`](spi-1161/q2-openai-paste-durable.json) records the first paste failing to parse
  (`Invalid URL string`, before the probe accepted other forms); [`q2-openai-paste-durable-2.json`](spi-1161/q2-openai-paste-durable-2.json)
  records the shape of the one that parsed (`paste-shape`).

## Q2. ChatGPT token sharing (`openai-chatgpt`) with a pasted 127.0.0.1 redirect

**No-go as tested**, and the cause is not settled. Evidence:
[`q2-openai-paste.json`](spi-1161/q2-openai-paste.json) (round 3: pi-ai's flow already gone),
[`q2-openai-paste-durable.json`](spi-1161/q2-openai-paste-durable.json) (the paste that did not parse),
[`q2-openai-paste-durable-2.json`](spi-1161/q2-openai-paste-durable-2.json),
[`q2-openai-paste-durable-3.json`](spi-1161/q2-openai-paste-durable-3.json).

**What worked:**
- The authorize URL (`auth.openai.com/api/accounts/authorize`, `client_id=dynamic_agent_client`,
  `ext_agent_host_id=urn:uuid:<device id>`, `resource=https://api.openai.com/v1`, `scope=… resource.invoke
  chatgpt.tokens.use.direct`) let the owner approve from his phone.
- OpenAI then redirected to `http://127.0.0.1:1455/auth/callback`. The page failed to load, as expected. He copied the
  address bar, a 2109-character URL with `http://`, holding `client_id` (an issued `oaiapp_…`), `code`, `scope` and
  `state`. The state matched.
- So the redirect could be completed by a paste.

**What failed:** the exchange at `auth.openai.com/api/accounts/oauth/token` (form-encoded: `authorization_code`, the
issued client id, `code`, `code_verifier`, `redirect_uri`, `resource`), sent from the Worker, answered
`400 {"error":"invalid_grant"}` twice. The second time was a fresh flow, approved once and pasted at once (6.5 min
after its start). The first try was lost with pi-ai's in-memory flow, and the second (`-2`) reused an authorize URL
the owner had already approved once.

**Possible causes, which these attempts cannot tell apart:**
- **(a) Plan entitlement.** The owner is on ChatGPT Free, and token sharing (`chatgpt.tokens.use.direct`) may require a
  paid plan. OpenAI's consent page still issued a code, and the error body says nothing more than `invalid_grant`.
- **(b) A refusal of the Worker's request.** The same host's device-flow exchange (`/oauth/token`) worked from the
  Worker, and the endpoint answered with OpenAI's own JSON, not a challenge, so this looks less likely. But we never
  sent the exchange from any other egress.
- Code expiry or reuse explains the second attempt at most, not the third.

**No mutating check is left to us:** each code is single-use. To settle it, repeat the flow with a paid account, and
send the exchange from the laptop as well.

**Plan requirement (found after the probe):** coverage of OpenAI's "Sign in with ChatGPT" plan usage, which is this
flow (`chatgpt.tokens.use.direct`), says it is for Plus and Pro subscribers only (for example
[The New Stack, 2026-09-29](https://thenewstack.io/sign-in-with-chatgpt/)). That would make cause (a) the likely one;
it has not been checked against OpenAI's own docs or with a paid account.

**Recommendation:** leave ChatGPT token sharing out of the first sign-in tickets until a Plus or Pro account has
rechecked it (see escalation 2). Offer ChatGPT through the Codex backend (`openai-codex`), whose device code works.

### Custom-zone probe (2026-11-16): the zone is not the variable

Evidence: [`q3-codex-zone-probe.json`](spi-1161/q3-codex-zone-probe.json).

**Setup.**
- A standalone throwaway Worker, `codex-probe`, outside t3flare. It is not committed anywhere; the source was a scratch
  file and is summarised here.
- One script served at two hosts:
  - the Workers Custom Domain `codex-probe.ianpascoe.dev`, on the same zone as Commissary's `ai.ianpascoe.dev`;
  - `codex-probe.ian-g-pascoe.workers.dev`, as the control.
- It held the same code, the same token and one Durable Object for both hosts.
- Every route was gated by a bearer generated locally; only its SHA-256 was in the code.
- It ran ChatGPT's device flow from the Durable Object's alarm (`signIn` in the file). The token's
  `chatgpt_plan_type` is again `free`.
- Each run sent the same matrix to `chatgpt.com/backend-api/codex/responses`:
  - SSE: Commissary's exact set, the minimal set (`authorization, content-type, accept`, codex-tui user agent), the
    no-user-agent set, a bare `authorization, content-type, accept`, Commissary's set without `originator`, without
    `chatgpt-account-id` and without `session-id`/routing hint/`connection`, and pi-ai's set;
  - WebSocket handshakes: Commissary's exact set, the minimal set, and the no-user-agent set.
- Each matrix ran twice per host: once from the stateless fetch handler, once inside the Durable Object.
- Each run first fetched `httpbin.org/headers`, which echoes the subrequest's `cf-worker` header.
- The owner deployed it with wrangler. Uploading it through Executor's Cloudflare app failed four times, each with an
  HTTP 400 whose body Executor does not show (Executor call refs `call_h3aaj2zaen0sag`, `call_46s3y1rj4hvrmf`,
  `call_ec0beepkaqcv39`, `call_3dgfe7zrtczvtq`); nothing was created by those attempts.

**Results.**

| Host | Run in | `cf-worker` seen by httpbin | SSE (8 variants) | WebSocket (3 handshakes) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `codex-probe.ianpascoe.dev` | fetch handler | **`ianpascoe.dev`** | all 403, `server: cloudflare`, 2–3 ms | all 403, 1–2 ms |
| `codex-probe.ianpascoe.dev` | Durable Object | `ian-g-pascoe.workers.dev` | all 403, 1–3 ms | all 403, 2 ms |
| `codex-probe.ian-g-pascoe.workers.dev` (control) | fetch handler | `ian-g-pascoe.workers.dev` | all 403, 2–3 ms | all 403, 2 ms |
| `codex-probe.ian-g-pascoe.workers.dev` (control) | Durable Object | `ian-g-pascoe.workers.dev` | all 403, 2–3 ms | all 403, 1–2 ms |

- **The zone is not the variable.** A subrequest that carried `cf-worker: ianpascoe.dev`, the zone Commissary's API
  Worker runs on, was blocked exactly like the `workers.dev` control. No header set passes from either host, so there
  is no minimal passing set to name.
- **A Durable Object's subrequests carry `cf-worker: <account>.workers.dev`** even when the request came in on the
  custom domain. A Durable Object cannot take on its caller's zone, so t3flare's Session DOs would always present
  `workers.dev`; that turned out not to matter here.
- **The Free plan is not the variable either.** An earlier grant on the same Free-plan account passed from the laptop
  on 2026-11-15 ([`q3-codex-backend-laptop.json`](spi-1161/q3-codex-backend-laptop.json)); this probe's grant never
  left the Durable Object, so it was not re-run from the laptop. The probe's grant was blocked from both hosts in about 2 ms. That is too fast for OpenAI's origin
  to have looked at the token: the refusal comes from the edge in front of chatgpt.com.
- `postman-echo.com` showed no `cf-*` headers at all: it strips them, so only httpbin's echo counts.
- **The grant was revoked** from the probe (`revoke` in the file: 200 for both tokens, then a refresh answered 401
  "Your session has ended"). Observed, not recorded: `/status` on both hosts then read `tokenHeld: false`. The owner
  deletes the Worker and its custom domain (`wrangler delete --name codex-probe` from the scratch directory).

**What we still cannot explain.** Commissary appears to reach the Codex backend from a Worker on the same zone; its
headers here come from reading its code at `8a6298cd1`, not the live deploy, and that it uses the same Cloudflare
account is not evidenced. The remaining differences are untested:
- Commissary's credential is not the owner's Free-plan grant.
- Its compatibility date is 2026-08-01, with `nodejs_compat` and `global_fetch_strictly_public`
  (`alchemy.run.ts:38`). The probe used compatibility date 2026-11-01 with no flags, so the custom zone combined
  with `global_fetch_strictly_public` was never tried.
- Its upstream URL is set per credential (`base_url`), and its responses do not show which one served the request.
  It serves `gpt-6-luna`, which only the Codex backend has, so some credential of it does reach that backend, but not
  necessarily from its Worker at `chatgpt.com/backend-api/codex`.

The edge may also treat something about our requests differently that we did not vary: the body, TLS, or the time.

**What the Codex tickets must decide (escalation):** a Durable agent's ChatGPT turns, and the relay's Codex route,
cannot call `chatgpt.com` from our Workers, on workers.dev or on the owner's zone. See escalation 1.

## Q4. A Worker pass-through that swaps a handle for the real credential

**The setup:**
- Each CLI ran in docker `ubuntu:26.04` (arm64, Codex's musl binary, Claude Code from npm), holding only a 32-byte
  handle (`t3h_…`).
- The Worker route looked up the handle's SHA-256 in the probe DO, got the current access token from
  `Models.getAuth` (which refreshes when near expiry), and set `Authorization: Bearer <token>`.
- It dropped the client's `authorization`, `x-api-key`, `host`, `cf-*` and `content-length` headers, and streamed the
  upstream body back through a timing `TransformStream`.

**Claude Code to Anthropic through the Worker: go.** Evidence:
[`q4-claude-code-worker-relay.json`](spi-1161/q4-claude-code-worker-relay.json).
- **Config:** `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL=<host>/api/probe1161/relay/anthropic` and `ANTHROPIC_AUTH_TOKEN=<handle>`. Claude
  sends it as `Authorization: Bearer`, and reports `apiKeySource: none`.
- **Tool turn:** 69 s wall time over 6 model requests. The idle gaps from two `sleep 25` Bash steps fell **between**
  requests, not inside a stream.
- **Single long stream:** `claude-sonnet-5-5` at high effort ran **90.8 s in one response** through the Worker (276 KB,
  first byte 0.4 s). The longest gap inside that stream was 1.6 s, so no long idle inside one stream was tested.
- **What Claude Code sends:**
  - `user-agent: claude-cli/2.1.322 (external, sdk-cli)`.
  - `anthropic-beta`: `interleaved-thinking-2025-05-14, context-management-2025-06-27, claude-code-20250219,
    per-turn-control-2026-07-01, mid-conversation-tool-changes-2026-07-01, thinking-token-count-2026-05-13,
    advanced-tool-use-2025-11-20, effort-2025-11-24, thinking-display-updates-2026-08-18`. It does **not** send
    `oauth-2025-04-20`, because it thinks it holds an API key.
  - Its own system prompt, as an array of blocks. The relay's check of whether the first block starts with "You are
    Claude Code" read **false on all 7 requests**, and all 7 got 200. What Claude Code puts first, and whether
    Anthropic looks for the identity anywhere in it, was not recorded.
- **What the relay added:** `Authorization: Bearer <oat>`, and `oauth-2025-04-20` appended to `anthropic-beta`. That
  combination worked. Whether the beta is required was not isolated (see the matrix).

**What Anthropic needs with an OAuth token**
([`q4-anthropic-header-matrix.json`](spi-1161/q4-anthropic-header-matrix.json)):

| Request (16 output tokens, "Say ok.") | Result |
|---|---|
| Bearer, no beta, no system | **429** |
| Bearer + `oauth-2025-04-20`, no system | **429** |
| Bearer + `oauth-2025-04-20` + `system` **equal to** "You are Claude Code, Anthropic's official CLI for Claude." (a plain string) | 200 |
| Bearer + `oauth-2025-04-20` + another system string | **429** |
| Bearer + both betas + the identity string | 200 |
| The same, plus a `claude-cli` user agent and `x-app: cli` | 200 |
| The token as `x-api-key` + `oauth-2025-04-20` | 401 `invalid x-api-key` |

The 429 is `rate_limit_error` "Error": a disguised refusal, not a real rate limit.

**What this does and does not establish:**
- The identity was tested only as the **whole** system string. The case of a system that starts with it and goes on,
  or has it as the first of several blocks (pi-ai's form), was not in the matrix.
- Claude Code's own requests passed while the relay's "first block starts with the identity" check read false.
- **The exact rule is therefore unestablished**, and SPI-1171 should test it: whole string, prefix, first block, or
  something else Claude Code sends (its betas, or a block the relay did not inspect).
- There was **no "identity, no beta" case**, so whether `oauth-2025-04-20` is needed was not isolated either.
- What is established: an OAuth token is accepted only as `Authorization: Bearer`; a request with the beta and the
  identity as its whole system passes; Claude Code's own requests pass through the relay with the beta added.

**Codex CLI to ChatGPT's Codex backend through the Worker: no-go** (the same block page,
[`q4-codex-worker-relay.json`](spi-1161/q4-codex-worker-relay.json)). The Codex config used:
`model_providers.relay={base_url="<host>/api/probe1161/relay/codex", wire_api="responses", env_key="T3H",
requires_openai_auth=false, supports_websockets=false}`. Codex sent its handle as a Bearer token, and `turn.failed`
with the 403 HTML.

**The same swap from non-Worker egress: go.** Evidence:
[`q4-codex-offworker-relay.json`](spi-1161/q4-codex-offworker-relay.json). A node relay in a second docker container
**on the laptop** did the swap, so these runs used the laptop's residential egress. None ran from a Workspace
Container.
- **Tool turn:** 75 s over 4 requests, with idle gaps from two `sleep 25` steps.
- **Single long stream:** high reasoning ran **123 s in one response** (1.6 MB, 6,780 output tokens including 1,621
  reasoning tokens, one idle gap of 5.0 s), ending with the marker.

**What the Codex CLI sends:**
- `originator: codex_exec` and `user-agent: codex_exec/0.167.2 (…)`.
- `session-id`, `thread-id`, `x-client-request-id`, `x-codex-window-id`, `x-codex-beta-features`,
  `x-codex-turn-metadata` (a JSON holding the installation, session and turn ids, the model and the sandbox mode),
  and `x-openai-internal-codex-responses-lite`.
- **The body** carries `client_metadata, include, input, model, parallel_tool_calls, prompt_cache_key, reasoning,
  store (false), stream (true), text, tool_choice`, about 52 KB for a first request.

**What the Codex backend needs** (matrix from the laptop, [`q3-codex-backend-laptop.json`](spi-1161/q3-codex-backend-laptop.json)):
with one header dropped at a time, `chatgpt-account-id`, `originator` and `OpenAI-Beta` were each optional for this
one-workspace account. A request with all three dropped together was not sent. The relay should still set
`chatgpt-account-id` from the token's claim, because an account in several workspaces needs it to pick one; pi-ai
always sends it.

**Streaming and idle gaps:** neither relay buffered. A Worker kept a 91 s response open with no limit hit, and gaps
between requests (25 s and more) cost nothing, because each request is its own fetch.

## Q5. Refresh-token rotation

Evidence: [`q5-anthropic-refresh.json`](spi-1161/q5-anthropic-refresh.json),
[`q5-anthropic-replay.json`](spi-1161/q5-anthropic-replay.json),
[`q5-openai-codex-refresh.json`](spi-1161/q5-openai-codex-refresh.json),
[`q5-openai-codex-replay.json`](spi-1161/q5-openai-codex-replay.json).

| | Anthropic (Claude OAuth) | ChatGPT (`openai-codex`) | Copilot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Access token lifetime | 8 h (`expires_in` 28800; pi-ai stores it 5 min short) | 10 days (`expires_in` 864000; the JWT's `exp − iat` agrees) | untested. In pi-ai's code, the Copilot token comes from `copilot_internal/v2/token` with its own `expires_at`, and the GitHub OAuth token serves as the refresh token and never rotates. |
| Refresh frequency (pi-ai refreshes at < 5 min left) | about every 8 h of use | about every 10 days | — |
| Does the refresh token rotate? | **Every refresh** | **Every refresh** (its length changed 196 → 211) | — |
| The replaced refresh token presented again | **Rejected**: `400 invalid_grant` "Refresh token not found or invalid". The current token still worked afterwards: no family revocation. | **Accepted**: it rotated again, and the current token also still worked. Reuse is tolerated at least seconds after a rotation. A longer window was not tested. | — |
| Cost of a lost rotation (refreshed at the vendor, but the new token never saved) | **The stored refresh token is dead, and the owner signs in again.** The access token from that refresh is lost too. | Probably recoverable within a short grace window. Beyond it, unknown, so treat it as signing in again. | — |
| Refresh-token lifetime | The token response has a `refresh_token_expires_in` field (its presence is recorded in [`q1-anthropic-paste-durable.json`](spi-1161/q1-anthropic-paste-durable.json); its value was not recorded; pi-ai 1.5.0 does not read it) | Not stated | — |

**What this means for the sign-in tickets:** the Environment DO as the only refresher (ADR-0018) matters most for
Claude.
- Write the refreshed credential to storage **before** answering any caller. pi-ai's `refreshStoredOAuthCredential`
  does this inside `modify`, so the DO's `CredentialStore.modify` must commit before it resolves; the probe's did.
- Nothing may refresh outside that lock.
- A deploy that resets the DO between Anthropic's answer and the write costs a sign-in.

## Escalations

1. **ChatGPT's Codex backend blocks our Workers, whatever the headers and whatever the zone.** Commissary's header set
   did not help, `global_fetch_strictly_public` did not, and a Worker on the owner's zone (`cf-worker: ianpascoe.dev`)
   was blocked like `workers.dev`. Commissary appears to get through, for reasons not found. This affects SPI-1164 (the
   ChatGPT sign-in for the Durable agent) and the relay's Codex route (SPI-1167 onwards). Options:
   - **Find Commissary's difference.** Read its live Codex credential's `base_url` and its upstream logs
     (`x-cpa-trace-id`, in Commissary's `packages/models/src/http/headers.ts`), rerun the probe matrix with
     Commissary's own credential, and try the custom zone with `global_fetch_strictly_public`. This needs
     Commissary's management access.
   - **Non-Worker egress.** This is untested beyond reaching chatgpt.com's edge: the Container test was an
     unauthenticated 401, and the 123 s turn ran from the laptop, not a Container. **As written before, this fallback
     put the token inside the Container, against ADR-0016.** It is acceptable only if the token is added by an egress
     proxy outside the agent's process. Two candidates:
     - the Workspace DO's outbound intercept (computer's `interceptOutboundHttp`), if that path leaves through the
       Container rather than the Worker, which itself needs a check;
     - a separate egress hop the owner runs.
   - **Drop ChatGPT subscriptions for Durable agents and the Codex relay** until one of the above is shown, and keep
     ChatGPT reachable only through an endpoint such as Commissary.
2. **ChatGPT token sharing** (`api.openai.com`) is unsettled: Free plan or Worker. This is OpenAI's "Sign in with
   ChatGPT" plan usage, and press coverage of its launch says it is for Plus and Pro subscribers only (not
   checked against OpenAI's own docs), which would explain the owner's Free-plan `invalid_grant`. It calls
   `api.openai.com`, not `chatgpt.com`, so the edge block in escalation 1 may not apply to it. Recommendation:
   recheck with a Plus or Pro account before deciding escalation 1; if it works from a Worker, it is the ChatGPT
   route for Durable agents.
3. **Copilot** is untested (its subscription has ended). The device flow itself runs in the Worker.
4. **Claude has no API sign-out.** The panel's "Sign out" can only forget the credential, and should send the owner
   to claude.ai to revoke.
